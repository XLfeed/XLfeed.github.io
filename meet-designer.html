Chapter 01 / The Inspiration

Where It All Began

My journey towards game design began with a particular moment in Tak 2: The Staff of Dreams.

Near the end of the game, in an area known as the Wanderlands, I reached the highest point of the level. Getting there required effort, but what awaited me was a breathtaking view of the surrounding world beneath an enormous, star-filled sky.

It wasn't a boss fight or a complicated gameplay mechanic that left the strongest impression on me. It was the environment itself.

I remember admiring the scenery and realising that perhaps, one day, I could create a world just as visually impressive. A world that other players could explore, experience, and remember.

That moment became one of the driving forces behind my decision to pursue game design.

Why This Moment Stayed With Me

Looking back, I think part of what made that experience so memorable was the journey to reach the viewpoint.

It felt like a reward for exploration. I had reached somewhere meaningful through my own actions, and the environment gave me a reason to stop and appreciate the world.

Today, I want to create experiences that leave players with that same sense of wonder.

I'm a student at SIT–DigiPen, studying Computer Science in Interactive Media and Game Development.

Chapter 02 / Design Philosophy

Clarity. Flow. Function.

My approach to design is guided by three principles.

01

Player Clarity

Players should be able to understand and learn a game's mechanics through play, without excessive instruction.

02

Engagement through Flow

Levels should feel natural to navigate, with deliberate pacing and progressively developing challenges.

03

Function-Driven Aesthetics

Visual presentation and environmental layout should support gameplay functionality, not distract from it.

Chapter 03 / Design and Code

From Concept to Implementation

One of the most rewarding parts of game development is taking a design idea and building the systems that make it possible.

In Undercooked, for example, I developed a dynamic pathfinding system that allows tower placement to affect enemy movement routes, together with placement validation to prevent players from blocking all available paths.

Experiences like these have shaped my interest in technical game design: understanding not only what a gameplay feature should accomplish, but how to implement it.
